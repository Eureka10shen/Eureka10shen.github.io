My research develops computational methods at the intersection of **quantum chemistry**, **scientific machine learning**, and **combustion modelling**. The common objective is to improve the accuracy, efficiency, or physical consistency of simulation-based methods.

## Neural quantum states for quantum chemistry

I study neural-network representations of electronic wavefunctions and optimization strategies for neural quantum states. This work aims to make high-accuracy *ab initio* calculations more practical while preserving the underlying quantum-mechanical structure.

## Data-driven combustion modelling

I develop sparse-learning methods for reducing detailed chemical reaction mechanisms. The goal is to remove computational redundancy while retaining the predictive accuracy required for combustion simulations.

My combustion research also includes experimental and modelling studies of metal-particle combustion, including aluminum particles modified with graphene fluoride and iron nanopowder additives in thermoacoustic systems.

## Physics-informed scientific machine learning

I investigate generative and inverse-modelling methods that incorporate physical constraints into scientific machine learning. One line of collaborative work uses diffusion-bridge models for physics-aligned field reconstruction and was presented as an ICLR 2025 Spotlight paper.

The [Publications](/publications/) page lists selected published work. Ongoing projects are not described here until their results are publicly available.
